Independence and governance

Applicants receive no cash payments, and no consideration is requested in exchange for support. Reviewers receive a fixed honorarium for completed review work, independent of the review outcome. Support is not associated with the prescription, promotion or preference of any product. Clinicians retain control of their research topic, findings and journal selection.

Read the full independence, ethics and authorship policy.

Frequently asked questions

Is programme support provided without charge?

Yes. Selected projects receive support in kind from CLINICTOPICS, without a fee or cash payment to the applicant.

Must institutional ethics approval be in place before applying?

No. An application may be submitted with a synopsis. The required institutional ethics approval must, however, be in place before any study activity begins.

Who can access my application?

Access is limited to CLINICTOPICS personnel and reviewers involved in assessment, who are bound by confidentiality. Applications are not shared with companies or other sponsors.

How is authorship determined?

Authorship is determined according to recognised criteria. Contributors are acknowledged for their assistance and included as authors only when they meet the applicable authorship criteria.

What happens if my project is not selected?

You will receive the reason for the decision. You may revise and resubmit, or consider the corresponding paid service; neither option is required.

What support is available if I cannot meet the service fee?

Clinicians facing financial barriers may contact us to discuss an access token, which may waive a fee or provide an invitation to the support programme. Enter an issued token on the application or review-request form. Tokens affect access or payment only and do not influence scientific review. See the token rules.

Can publication be guaranteed?

No. Publication decisions are made by journals. We support the preparation of a rigorous, well-presented submission but cannot guarantee acceptance.

Who is eligible to apply?

Registered medical practitioners in private practice, private hospitals or government hospitals may apply. Institutional permission may be required for clinicians employed by government institutions.

What is the expected timeframe?

We aim to communicate a decision within four weeks of the closing date for each application round. Delivery timelines depend on the study design and the institutional ethics-review schedule.

Do companies select or fund programme projects?

No. CLINICTOPICS provides support in kind, independently of products, brands or companies. Companies do not have access to applications.

How is my manuscript protected?

Reviewers agree in writing to maintain confidentiality. For pre-submission review, author-identifying details are removed before the manuscript is shared with the reviewer.

How the programme works

All applications are considered through the same defined process.

  1. Submit your synopsis

    Submit the application form with your professional details and a concise study synopsis.

  2. Verification

    We verify professional registration and institutional details and screen for duplicate or overlapping proposals.

  3. Expert review

    Reviewers assess scientific merit, feasibility, ethics readiness, clinical relevance and programme capacity. All reviewers are required to declare relevant conflicts of interest.

  4. Decision

    Applicants receive one of four decisions: selected for support, offered a paid service, invited to revise and resubmit, or declined with an explanation.

  5. Support agreement

    The written agreement specifies the services to be provided, confirms that support is in kind and that the clinician retains control of the results, and sets out the applicable authorship and funding disclosures.

  6. Ethics approval

    The study must receive approval from the relevant institutional ethics committee before research activities begin. We can assist with preparation of the submission materials.

  7. Guided delivery

    Support may include protocol development, statistical and data management, manuscript preparation, journal selection and submission, and responses to reviewers. The clinician reviews and approves the work at each stage.

Timelines and what to expect

We aim to communicate a decision within four weeks of the closing date for each application round. Delivery timelines depend on the study design and the institutional ethics-review schedule. The agreed plan will be documented in the support agreement.

Token rules

How access tokens work, who can get one, and what they do not do.

An access token is a code issued by CLINICTOPICS that may waive the fee for a paid review or provide direct entry to the support programme. Tokens are intended to address financial barriers to research support; they do not influence the conduct or outcome of a review.

The two kinds of token

Payment waiverSupport invitation
PurposeWaives the fee for an eligible paid reviewProvides access to the support programme without waiting for a selection round
Where you enter itOn the "Request a review" formOn the application form
What it coversAny review, Quick check only, or Expert review only, as stated when it is issuedOne support application
What still appliesThe review itself, conducted according to the standard processVerification of your registration, ethics approval, and the support agreement

The rules

  • Tokens are issued only by CLINICTOPICS, and the reason for each issuance is recorded.
  • Each token has a set number of uses and may have an expiry date.
  • Each token is issued to a named clinician or group and must not be shared, sold or transferred.
  • Tokens have no cash value and cannot be redeemed for money.
  • A payment waiver works only for the review type stated on it.
  • A token becomes invalid when its permitted uses are exhausted, it expires or it is revoked.
  • CLINICTOPICS may revoke a token if it was issued in error or is misused.

What a token never does

  • A token does not affect the review process or its findings.
  • Reviewers are not informed whether an access token was used.
  • A token does not waive ethics approval, registration verification or the support agreement.
  • A token does not guarantee that a study will proceed or that a journal will accept a manuscript.
  • It is never linked to any product, company or outcome.

Requesting an access token

Tokens are not sold or advertised. Clinicians experiencing a financial barrier, or those who have received an invitation, may contact us through the contact form. We consider each request on its own and cannot promise a token.

Using an access token

  1. Find the token field

    It is on the application form and on the "Request a review" form, marked optional.

  2. Enter your code

    Token codes are not case-sensitive and may be entered with or without spaces. A code follows the format CT-XXXX-XXXX.

  3. Check the confirmation

    If valid, the token waives the applicable payment or selection step and a confirmation is displayed. If it cannot be used, the message will indicate whether it is unrecognised, exhausted, expired, revoked or inapplicable to the request.

Fairness and records

Records include the recipient, reason for issue, relevant dates and usage history. These records support equitable issuance and appropriate use and are handled in accordance with the privacy policy. Misuse may result in revocation and ineligibility for future tokens.

How the programme works

  1. Request a review

    Upload the manuscript, specify the intended journal and select either Quick Check or Expert Review.

  2. Payment

    Complete payment online or enter an eligible access token. Applicable discounts are applied automatically.

  3. Assignment

    A CLINICTOPICS editor conducts an initial file check. For Expert Review, an external reviewer with relevant expertise and no declared conflict of interest is assigned.

  4. Review

    The reviewer completes a structured assessment. Author-identifying details are removed before review.

  5. Your report

    The editor consolidates the assessment into a report, which is made available through your portal. You may revise the manuscript and request a further review.

Confidentiality

Unpublished manuscripts are treated as confidential. Reviewers agree in writing not to copy, disclose or reuse submitted material. Reviewers receive a fixed honorarium for each completed review, irrespective of its outcome, and receive no payment from companies. You will be informed before any software other than word-processing or similarity-checking tools is used in relation to your manuscript.

Become a reviewer

Experienced clinicians and researchers may be invited to review. Reviewers provide a professional profile, declare relevant conflicts of interest, sign a confidentiality agreement and complete one paid trial review. A fixed fee is paid for each completed review. See Become a reviewer.

Protocol development

A clear, methodologically sound protocol supports ethical review and subsequent publication. We assist in preparing a protocol that is sufficiently detailed for investigators and review committees.

Scope of services

  • Sharpening your research question and objectives
  • Choosing the study design and the reporting guideline that fits it
  • Defining the population, setting, and inclusion and exclusion criteria
  • Defining outcomes and the data you will collect
  • Sample size reasoning, with our statistician
  • An outline of the statistical analysis plan
  • Drafts of the participant information sheet and consent form
  • Preparing your ethics-committee submission and replying to the committee's queries

Information required from you

  • Your initial concept or synopsis, at any stage of development
  • Details of the clinical setting and anticipated participant numbers
  • The applicable ethics committee forms and submission requirements
  • Any data or literature you are building on
  • Timely review and approval of each draft

Deliverables

  • A protocol document prepared under your direction
  • An analysis plan outline
  • Draft information sheet and consent form
  • A complete ethics submission pack
  • A checklist of approvals and registrations your study may need

Outside the scope

  • We do not obtain ethics approval on your behalf; approval is granted or declined by the relevant committee.
  • Start or run your study
  • Create or adjust data
  • Promise that a protocol will be approved

Service process

  1. Question and design

    We define the research question, study design and applicable reporting guideline with you.

  2. Draft protocol

    We prepare each section using your input; you review and provide comments.

  3. Consent and information documents

    We prepare draft documents for adaptation to the committee’s required format.

  4. Submission pack

    We assemble the submission materials and assist in preparing responses to committee queries.

Regulatory and ethics approvals. Ethics committee approval is required before a biomedical research study begins. If your study is a clinical trial, it must also be registered in advance with the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI), before the first participant is enrolled. Requirements may change; we identify those that may apply, and you should confirm them with your institution. See the ethics submission guide.

Your role and responsibilities

You retain authorship and direct the content. You review and approve the work at each stage. Our contribution is disclosed in accordance with applicable authorship criteria, as described in the authorship guide.

Statistics and data management

Good analysis starts before the data arrive. We support data-management planning, data collection, data cleaning and statistical analysis, and prepare clear interpretations of the results.

Scope of services

  • Sample size calculation and a written justification, using your assumptions
  • Data collection sheet design and a data dictionary
  • Data entry templates, coding and validation checks
  • Data cleaning and a record of every change made
  • A statistical analysis plan
  • The analysis itself, matched to your design and outcomes
  • Tables and figures ready for the manuscript
  • Methods and results text for you to review

Information required from you

  • Your protocol or objectives
  • Your data, in an agreed format, with all patient-identifying details removed
  • Definitions for every variable
  • Your assessment of the clinical relevance of the outcomes

Deliverables

  • An analysis plan
  • A clean, de-identified dataset and data dictionary
  • Analysis outputs, tables and figures
  • Draft statistical methods and results text
  • A short note on limitations

Outside the scope

  • Analyse data that identify patients. Please remove names, contact details and hospital numbers first.
  • Change, remove or create data to reach a particular result
  • Choose analyses to fit a conclusion decided in advance
  • Replace your clinical judgement about what the results mean

Service process

  1. Plan

    We agree the analysis plan and sample size before data collection ends.

  2. Prepare data

    We check, clean and document your dataset.

  3. Analyse

    We run the agreed analyses, and any extra analyses you request, clearly marked as such.

  4. Report

    We prepare tables, figures and accompanying text, and discuss the findings with you.

Journal selection and submission

Appropriate journal selection can reduce avoidable delays. We assess potential journals for scope, readership and publishing practices, then help prepare a submission package that meets the journal’s requirements.

Scope of services

  • A reasoned shortlist of journals assessed for scope, readership, article type, indexing and fees
  • Assessment for indicators of deceptive or predatory publishing practices
  • A compliance check against the journal's author guidelines
  • Cover letter, title page, declarations and file preparation
  • Guidance through the journal's online submission system
  • Advice on what to do if the paper is declined

Information required from you

  • Your final manuscript
  • Your priorities, including publication timelines, open access, indexing and cost
  • Your publication-fee parameters and any waivers for which you may be eligible
  • We do not require your journal-account credentials; you retain control of your own account.

Deliverables

  • A journal shortlist with our reasoning
  • A submission-ready package
  • A step-by-step guide to the submission system
  • A submission checklist

Outside the scope

  • Guarantee acceptance, or a fast decision
  • Recommend a journal in exchange for payment or another benefit; we receive no such benefit from journals or publishers.
  • Submit under our name. You remain the corresponding author.

Service process

  1. Shortlist

    We assess candidate journals against the manuscript and your stated priorities.

  2. Check

    We assess each journal’s publishing practices and review its author instructions.

  3. Prepare

    We prepare the files and declarations required by the journal.

  4. Submit

    We guide you through the submission process; you submit the manuscript using your own account.

Response to reviewers

A request for revision provides an opportunity to address editorial and reviewer concerns. We assess the comments, plan appropriate revisions and prepare a clear, respectful, evidence-based response.

Scope of services

  • Organising every reviewer comment in a table
  • Categorising comments by required revision, optional revision and matters for a reasoned response or disagreement
  • A revision plan, including any extra analyses
  • A point-by-point response letter, drafted for your review
  • Checking the revised manuscript for consistency with the reply
  • A tracked-changes version and a clean version
  • Advice on how and when to ask an editor to reconsider

Information required from you

  • The editor's decision letter and all reviewer comments
  • Your original submission and the data
  • Your view on each comment
  • Sufficient time to review and approve the response before resubmission

Deliverables

  • A comments matrix
  • A revision plan
  • A draft response letter
  • Revised manuscript files ready to resubmit

Outside the scope

  • Answer reviewers without your input
  • Change your results to satisfy a comment
  • Guarantee that a revised paper will be accepted

Service process

  1. Sort

    We catalogue each comment and agree on an appropriate response.

  2. Plan

Writing your first synopsis

A structured guide to preparing a concise and complete study synopsis.

A synopsis summarises a proposed study for review by an ethics committee, supervisor or programme panel. It should be clear, specific and complete; approximately 250–400 words is usually sufficient.

  1. Title

    State the design and the population, not just the topic. "A retrospective cohort study of X outcomes in adults with Y" says more than "A study of Y".

  2. Background

    Summarise the established evidence, the remaining knowledge gap and its relevance to clinical practice.

  3. Objectives

    Specify one primary objective and, where appropriate, secondary objectives that are specific and measurable.

  4. Methods

    Describe the design, setting, eligibility criteria, primary outcome and its measurement, and anticipated sample size.

  5. Analysis

    Describe the planned analysis of the primary outcome, including the proposed statistical test or model where known.

  6. Ethics

    Describe the required ethics review, the consent process or rationale for a waiver, and the measures planned to protect confidentiality.

  7. Expected value

    Describe the potential implications of the findings and the stakeholders who may benefit.

  8. Timeline and resources

    Provide an estimated study timeline and identify available resources, including participants, data, equipment and personnel time.

Common mistakes

  • A research question that is too broad for the available data
  • Failure to define a primary outcome, or specifying multiple competing primary outcomes
  • A sample-size estimate without a stated rationale or calculation
  • Omitting the ethics-review pathway or assuming that retrospective research is automatically exempt
  • Including patient names or other identifiers in the synopsis
  • Drawing conclusions before data collection and analysis are complete

Before you send it

  • Could a clinician outside your specialty understand the synopsis readily?
  • Are all numerical values and technical terms defined?
  • Is the study design appropriate to the research question?
  • Have all patient-identifying details been removed?

Preparing an ethics-committee submission

Common ethics-submission requirements and measures to support a complete application.

Ethics committee approval protects participants, and it protects you. Journals ask whether a study was approved, and many will not consider a paper that was not. Requirements depend on your institution and your study, so treat this page as a general guide and confirm the details with your own ethics committee.

The basics

  • Obtaining ethics committee approval before starting a biomedical research study is a requirement under India's national guidelines.
  • The national guidance is the ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants (2017). Many committees base their forms on it.
  • Depending on the risk and the type of study, a committee may exempt it from review, use a shorter (expedited) review, or review it at a full committee meeting. Your committee decides which applies.
  • If your study is a clinical trial, prospective registration with the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI) is mandatory, and should be done before the first participant is enrolled. The 2017 guidelines describe registration of other research as voluntary. Check the current rules.
  • Ethics committees that review biomedical research are themselves expected to be registered under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019. If you are outside an institution, ask about independent ethics committees.

What committees usually ask for

  • A covering letter and the committee's application form
  • The full protocol, with the study design, methods and analysis plan
  • The participant information sheet and consent form, and translations where participants need them
  • The data collection sheet or questionnaire
  • Investigators' CVs, declarations and any required training certificates
  • A statement of funding and conflicts of interest
  • Permission letters from the institution or department, where required

Avoiding delays

  • Read the committee's checklist before you write, and match its format
  • Explain clearly how you will protect confidentiality and store data
  • Say whether you are asking for a waiver of consent, and justify it
  • Check every document is signed, dated and consistently titled
  • Submit early. Committees meet on fixed dates

Case reports. Journals usually require the patient's written consent for publication of a case report, and many ask whether the institution needs to be told. Get it before you write.

Choosing a journal, and spotting predatory ones

A structured journal-assessment checklist for authors preparing a submission.

An appropriate journal reaches the intended readership and applies credible editorial and peer-review standards. Unsuitable journal selection may result in avoidable delay, expense or reputational harm. Apply the following checks before submission.

  1. Know your article type and audience

    Identify the article type (for example, original research, brief report, case report or review) and the intended readership, such as specialists, generalists or policymakers.

  2. Read the aims and scope

    Review recent issues to determine whether the journal publishes work of a similar scope and article type. If it does not, consider a more suitable journal.

  3. Check where it is indexed

    Look for indexing in databases such as PubMed, Scopus or Web of Science, and check it yourself on those databases, not on the journal's own website. Open access journals can be checked on the Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ).

  4. Check who runs it

    Check that the editorial board is identifiable and verifiable, the peer-review process is described, contact details are functional, and fees are disclosed before submission.

  5. Check its standards

    If a journal claims alignment with the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE), verify the claim using COPE’s official resources.

  6. Use a checklist

    The Think. Check. Submit. checklist can support assessment of journals with which you are unfamiliar.

  7. Ask a senior colleague

    Consult experienced colleagues in your specialty about journals with appropriate scope and editorial standards.

Warning signs of a predatory journal

  • Unsolicited solicitation that promises unusually rapid acceptance or uses excessive flattery
  • An implausibly broad scope or a journal name that closely resembles an established title
  • Publication charges disclosed only after acceptance
  • Impact-factor or indexing claims that cannot be independently verified
  • An absent or unverifiable editorial board, including members unaware that they are listed
  • Persistent language or factual errors on the journal website
  • No transparent description of the peer-review process

No single authoritative list identifies all predatory journals. Assess each journal using multiple independent indicators.

Authorship and acknowledgements

Authorship criteria and appropriate acknowledgement of contributions.

Authorship confers credit and entails accountability. Applying authorship criteria consistently protects the integrity of the record and the interests of all contributors.

Who is an author?

The International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) recommends that authorship rest on four criteria. Everyone who is an author should meet all four, and everyone who meets all four should be an author.

  1. A substantial contribution to the conception or design of the work, or to the acquisition, analysis or interpretation of data.
  2. Drafting the work, or revising it critically for important intellectual content.
  3. Final approval of the version to be published.
  4. Agreement to be accountable for all aspects of the work, so that questions about its accuracy or integrity are properly investigated and resolved.

People who do not meet all four

Contributors who do not meet all four criteria should be acknowledged rather than listed as authors. This may include individuals who provide supervised data collection, technical assistance, general support, or writing and editing support that does not meet the criteria. Obtain permission from each person named in the acknowledgements.

Things to avoid

  • Guest authorship: Including an individual who has not made a qualifying contribution, often on the basis of seniority
  • Ghost authorship: Omitting an individual who made a substantial qualifying contribution
  • Changing the author list late, without the agreement of all authors

Good practice

  • Agree the author list, order and individual contributions at the outset; review these if responsibilities change.
  • Designate a corresponding author to manage communication with the journal.
  • Use the journal’s required contributor statement; the CRediT taxonomy provides standardised role descriptions.
  • Disclose funding and all relevant support, including writing and editorial assistance.
  • Follow the journal’s policy on artificial-intelligence tools and make disclosures where required.

How we work. Our contribution is disclosed and does not replace the authors’ substantive input. Authors retain responsibility for the content and conclusions. See the independence and authorship policy.

Independence, ethics and authorship policy

Governance principles for independent, ethical and equitable support.

This policy describes the principles governing CLINICTOPICS support and applies to staff, reviewers and other programme contributors.

1. Support is given in kind

Support is provided in kind as services; applicants receive no cash or gifts. Pre-submission reviewers receive a fixed honorarium for completed review work, independent of the review outcome and any company.

2. No commercial links

Project selection and funding are independent of products, brands and companies. We accept no payment or benefit from journals or publishers in exchange for recommendations.

3. The doctor stays in control

The clinician retains control of study-design decisions, findings, conclusions and journal selection. Our support does not supersede these decisions.

4. Authorship

Authorship follows recognised criteria, described in our authorship guide. Contributions that do not meet these criteria are acknowledged rather than attributed as authorship.

5. Ethics first

Research activities begin only after the required institutional ethics approval. We can assist with submission preparation and do not seek to influence committee decisions.

6. Fair selection

Selection uses the same published criteria for every applicant. CLINICTOPICS may also invite a doctor directly with an access token. Every token is logged with a reason, and a token never affects the scientific review or its outcome. See the token rules.

7. Conflicts of interest

Staff and reviewers declare any conflict of interest. A person with a conflict does not participate in the relevant decision or review, and we assign the work to someone else.

8. Confidentiality

Unpublished manuscripts and application details are confidential. Reviewers are bound in writing not to copy, share, reuse or upload them elsewhere.

9. Disclosure

Every publication that receives our support carries a funding statement naming CLINICTOPICS, and relevant relationships are disclosed.

10. Complaints and review

Concerns may be submitted through our complaints route. This policy is reviewed periodically and updated when required.

  • To verify your registration and consider your application
  • To provide the services you ask for
  • To reply to your questions, quote requests and complaints
  • To check that tokens and selection are fair
  • To improve our services, using summaries that do not identify you
  • To meet legal obligations

Who sees it

  • Our team, who need it to do their work
  • Reviewers, under a written duty of confidentiality. Pre-submission reviewers see manuscripts with author details removed.
  • Service providers that help us run the site and services, such as hosting, email and payment providers, who may process data on our instructions
  • Authorities, if the law requires it
  • We do not sell your information. We do not share applications or manuscripts with pharmaceutical companies, sponsors or other third parties.

Account security and verification

Each member is required to verify an email address and a mobile number by means of a one-time code. The codes are valid for a limited period and permit a limited number of attempts. Passwords are not stored in readable form. Sessions expire automatically. You may download your account data, or delete your account, at any time from the account settings page.

Fonts and third-party services

This website retrieves fonts from Google Fonts, which means that your browser connects to Google’s servers when you visit. Some service providers may process information outside India.

How long we keep it

Information is retained only for as long as required for the purpose for which it was collected or as required by law. You may request deletion of your application or account. Retention periods for each record type will be stated before applications open.

How we protect it

We use secure connections, restrict access to authorised personnel and require staff and reviewers to maintain confidentiality. No system can be guaranteed to be completely secure; submit only information necessary for the requested service.

Your choices

  • Ask what information we hold about you
  • Request correction of inaccurate information
  • Request deletion of your information or restriction of its use
  • Withdraw consent where applicable. Withdrawal does not affect processing conducted before the request.
  • Raise a concern with us through the complaints route

Cookies

This site does not use advertising or tracking cookies.

Application data policy

How application information, manuscripts and submitted documents are handled.

Draft for legal review. This draft should not be relied upon until it has been reviewed and approved. The site owner can disable this notice in the site settings.

This policy explains how we handle the information in your application, and the manuscripts and documents you send us. It works together with our privacy policy.

What counts as application data

  • Your name, contact details, designation, institution and registration ID
  • Your synopsis and declarations
  • Manuscripts, data summaries and other documents you send
  • Our notes, scores and decisions about your application
  • Any access token you used

Who can see it

  • Authorised CLINICTOPICS personnel involved in application processing
  • Reviewers who score applications. Identifying details are removed from materials provided to reviewers where practicable. Reviewers declare any conflict of interest and are bound by confidentiality.
  • For pre-submission review, author-identifying details are removed, and reviewers are not informed whether a token or discount was used.

What we do with it

  • Verify your registration and institution
  • Check for duplicate or overlapping topics
  • Score and decide on your application using our published criteria
  • Provide the support or review you asked for

What we never do

  • Send your application or manuscript to any company, sponsor or other third party
  • Use your unpublished work for any other purpose
  • Sell or trade your information
  • Publish your work, or name you as a supported doctor, without your written consent

Reviewer applications

A reviewer application is visible only to authorised CLINICTOPICS personnel who conduct screening and verification. Referees are contacted only with the applicant’s consent and only after screening. The application is never shared with authors, other reviewers or third parties, and is not displayed on any public profile. An applicant may withdraw an application at any time, and may request deletion of the application and any uploaded curriculum vitae.

Uploads and correspondence through your account

Members who are selected for the support programme submit study documents and correspond with CLINICTOPICS through the secure portal, rather than through personal email addresses. Uploaded documents are associated with the relevant study, are visible only to the member and to authorised CLINICTOPICS personnel, and are never displayed on a public profile. Only published works that a member has chosen to display appear on a public profile.

Patient information

Do not include anything that identifies a patient. If identifiable information is submitted inadvertently, contact us promptly so that it can be deleted.

Keeping it safe

Access is restricted to personnel who require it. Files are transferred over secure connections. If online storage is enabled, the storage arrangements will be described.

Keeping and deleting it

Application data are retained only for as long as required for the purposes described above or as required by law. You may request deletion of an application or submitted files. A minimal record of the request and its handling may be retained.

Your rights

Requests to access, correct or delete personal data, or to withdraw consent, may be submitted through the contact page.

What this website is

This website describes CLINICTOPICS, which provides publication support to medical professionals. Services include assistance with study protocols, statistical methods, manuscript preparation, journal selection and pre-submission review. The website provides information about these services; it is not a medical journal, research publisher, regulator or ethics committee.

No medical, legal or regulatory advice

The website does not provide medical advice and must not be used to diagnose, treat or make decisions about a patient. It also does not constitute legal, ethical or regulatory advice. Clinicians remain responsible for complying with the requirements of their institution, ethics committee, relevant regulators and target journal.

No guarantee of results

We support the preparation of rigorous research and manuscripts but cannot guarantee study approval, journal acceptance or the absence of issues identified in review. Journals, ethics committees and institutions make their own decisions. Pre-submission review provides an expert opinion based on the material supplied and does not replace a journal’s peer-review process.

You remain the author

Authors are responsible for their data, analysis, conclusions and declarations, and for meeting recognised authorship criteria. The support we provide is acknowledged. It does not transfer authorship or responsibility to CLINICTOPICS or to any reviewer.

Independence, funding and access tokens

Support under this programme is provided in kind by CLINICTOPICS. It is not linked to any product, company or outcome. Reviewers receive a fixed honorarium for completing a review, whatever the result. CLINICTOPICS may issue access tokens, either to waive a fee or to invite a doctor into the support programme. A token affects access and payment only. It does not influence the scientific review, its findings or any journal's decision, and every token is recorded with a reason. See the token rules.

Confidentiality and patient information

Unpublished manuscripts and application details are handled confidentially and used only to provide the requested service. No system is completely secure. Do not upload patient-identifying information; remove names, contact details, hospital numbers and other identifiers before submitting a file, and ensure that all required institutional permissions and consents are in place.

Demonstration content

The portal demonstration uses sample names, publications, reports, tokens and amounts. Demonstration content does not represent actual records, prices or offers; information entered into the demonstration is not saved or transmitted. Prices and terms will be published before services commence.

Third-party names and links

Names of reporting guidelines, journals, regulators and standards are referenced for descriptive purposes and remain the property of their respective owners. Their mention does not imply endorsement by or of CLINICTOPICS. External links are provided for convenience; CLINICTOPICS is not responsible for the content of external websites.

Limits on our responsibility

To the extent permitted by law, CLINICTOPICS and its reviewers are not liable for loss arising from use of this website or reliance on a review, report or other material provided, including manuscript rejection or delay. Nothing in this disclaimer limits a right that cannot lawfully be limited.

Changes to this website

This website, its services and this disclaimer may be updated as the programme develops. Please review this page periodically. For enquiries, use the contact form.
